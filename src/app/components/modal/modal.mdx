## Live demo

On the web it opens in the middle of the screen; on a phone it slides up from the bottom. Switch the device to compare.

<DeviceCanvas of={ModalStories.Overview} defaultDevice="mobile" />

## When to use

**Use a modal** when people must deal with something before going on:

- Confirming something that can't be undone, like deleting.
- A short task that belongs to this page, like adding an address.
- Something that stops work until handled, like an expired session.

**Don't use one** for information people can read in passing (use a [Notification](?path=/docs/components-notification--docs)), or for long tasks (use a page).

### Compared with look-alikes

| Component | Blocks the page | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Modal** | Yes | A decision or short task now |
| [Notification](?path=/docs/components-notification--docs) | No | Telling people something happened |
| [Toggletip](?path=/docs/components-tooltip--docs) | No | A short explanation on request |
| A new page | Not a layer | Long or multi-step tasks |

## Anatomy

<DeviceCanvas of={ModalStories.Anatomy} noCode />

| # | Part | Shows when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Header** | Always. The title (the question or task, which names the modal for screen readers), and an optional `subtitle`. With `headerDivider`, a line under it. |
| 2 | **Close** | `dismissible` (default). |
| 3 | **Body** | Always. A sentence, details, or a short form. Scrolls if it's tall. |
| 4 | **Footer** | When there are buttons. With `footerBar` (default) it's a bar with a line above; without, the buttons sit right under the body. |
| 5 | **Buttons** | `primaryLabel` says what happens; `secondaryLabel` cancels and closes. On the right by default, or on the left with `buttonsAlign="start"`. |

## Variants

Two kinds, by what's inside.

<DeviceVariation title="Decision" of={ModalStories.VarDecision}>

A question with two answers, like "Delete photo?" with Delete and Cancel. Use `size="sm"`, and `primaryVariant="destructive"` when the answer deletes something.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="Short form" of={ModalStories.Overview}>

A few fields that belong to this page, like adding an address. Use `size="md"`. More than about 5 fields, or several steps? Use a page.

</DeviceVariation>

### Buttons

Three ways, by how much there is to read and do.

<DeviceVariation title="Footer bar" of={ModalStories.VarFooterBar}>

The buttons sit in a bar with a thin line above. Use it for **forms and anything that scrolls**: the line keeps the buttons apart from the content, and they stay put while the body scrolls.

- This is the default.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="Buttons, no footer bar" of={ModalStories.VarNoBar}>

The buttons sit right under a short message, with no line. Use it for **quick decisions** like "Delete photo?", where the message is a sentence and nothing scrolls.

- Add `[footerBar]="false"`.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="No buttons" of={ModalStories.VarNoButtons}>

Something to look at, like a photo's details or a preview. People close it with the × or <kbd>Esc</kbd>.

- Leave out `primaryLabel` and `secondaryLabel`. Keep it `dismissible`, since the × is the only way out.
- Not for saying something worked ("Saved"). Use a toast.

</DeviceVariation>

## Size

<DeviceVariation title="Small, medium, large" of={ModalStories.SizeSwitchable} sizeSwitch>

The width on the web. On phones, modals are always full width. Use the switch in the toolbar.

- **Small** (`sm`, 400px): decisions.
- **Medium** (`md`, 560px, default): short forms.
- **Large** (`lg`, 800px): content that needs room, like a preview.

</DeviceVariation>

## Modifiers

<DeviceVariation title="Full screen on phones" of={ModalStories.ModFullscreen}>

`mobile="fullscreen"` fills a phone's screen, so a form has room above the keyboard. Use it for forms; keep decisions as a sheet. On the web it looks the same either way.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="Subtitle" of={ModalStories.ModSubtitle}>

`subtitle` adds a line under the title, like the name of the item it's about.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="Header divider" of={ModalStories.ModHeaderDivider}>

`[headerDivider]="true"` adds a line under the header. Use it when the body is long or scrolls, so the title stays apart from the content. Pair it with the footer bar for a modal with a line at both ends.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="Buttons on the left" of={ModalStories.ModButtonsStart}>

`buttonsAlign="start"` puts the buttons on the left, with the **main button first** so it's read first. On the right (default), the main button is last, nearest the corner.

- Use one side across a product, so people know where to look.
- On phones the buttons always stack full width, main button on top.

</DeviceVariation>

<DeviceVariation title="Not dismissible" of={ModalStories.ModNotDismissible}>

`[dismissible]="false"` removes the close button, and Esc and clicking outside do nothing. Only when people truly must choose, like signing in again. Always leave one way out.

</DeviceVariation>

## Specs

Use the switches to see each kind on each device: the buttons (**Footer bar / No bar / No buttons**), the header (**No line / Line**), where the buttons sit (**Right / Left**), and **Mobile / Web**. Blue shows the padding of the header, body and footer. The numbers are measured from the code.

<SpecPictures of={ModalStories.SpecDrawing} />

- **Width on the web:** 400, 560 or 800px (`size`), centred. **On phones:** full width, sliding up from the bottom (or full screen).
- **Buttons on phones** stack full width, 8px apart, with the main one on top.

## Content

- **Title: the question or task,** like "Delete photo?" or "Add address". Not "Warning" or "Are you sure?".
- **Body: what happens,** in one or two sentences, especially anything that can't be undone.
- **Buttons: say what they do,** like "Delete" and "Keep editing". Never "Yes" and "No": people should know what a button does without reading the question.
- **Two buttons at most.** The main one says the action; the other cancels.

<DoDont>
  <Guideline type="do" of={ModalStories.DoSpecificButtons}>name the buttons after what they do.</Guideline>
  <Guideline type="dont" of={ModalStories.DontYesNo}>ask "Are you sure?" with Yes and No.</Guideline>
  <Guideline type="do" of={ModalStories.DoShort}>keep it to a decision people need to make now.</Guideline>
  <Guideline type="dont" of={ModalStories.DontModalForInfo}>stop people to say something worked. Use a toast.</Guideline>
</DoDont>

## Behavior

### Keyboard

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> / <kbd>Shift</kbd> <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Moves between the modal's controls; focus can't leave it |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Closes it, unless it isn't dismissible |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> | Presses the focused button |

When it opens, focus moves into it. When it closes, focus goes back to the button that opened it. The page behind can't be scrolled or reached.

### Mobile and web

| | Mobile (below 672px) | Web (672px and up) |
|---|---|---|
| Opens | Slides up from the bottom | Fades in, centred |
| Forms | `mobile="fullscreen"` for room above the keyboard | The same modal |
| Buttons | Stacked, full width, main button on top in thumb reach | Side by side at the right |
| Close | 44px tap area; tapping the backdrop also closes | Click outside or Esc |
| Clear of the home bar | Yes (safe area) | Not needed |

See [Mobile and web](?path=/docs/foundations-mobile-and-web--docs).

## Accessibility

When the delete modal opens, a screen reader reads:

<ScreenReader name="Delete photo?" role="dialog" state="modal" description="The photo will be removed from this report. This can’t be undone." />

- **Built on the browser's `<dialog>`:** focus stays inside, Esc closes it, and the page behind is hidden from screen readers.
- **Named by its title** with `aria-labelledby`.
- **Focus returns** to the button that opened it.
- **Reduce motion:** no slide or fade when people ask for less motion.
- **Checked automatically:** every modal story runs an accessibility scan in the tests, plus focus, Esc and button tests.
